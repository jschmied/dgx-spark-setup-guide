Speculative decoding · Qwen3.8-27B on GB10

Acceptance Decay

This plane was drawn to explain why quantizing a bad drafter cannot rescue it. It was right about that — and then DFlash2 landed below the leader on its vertical axis and won anyway.

What the plane got right

Shrinking DSpark's drafter from bf16 to 4-bit is the horizontal arrow: cost per step falls from 41.5 GB to 27.5, and the hit rate does not move at all — 62.4 % either way. The deficit was never bandwidth. Take the drafter to zero bytes and DSpark still yields 2.73 tokens per step against MTP's 3.31.

What it got wrong

The vertical axis is hit rate at the first draft position, and the caption read: the gap to MTP is vertical. DFlash2 sits 8.2 pp lower than MTP there — and delivers 4.05 tokens per step against 3.31, a 22 % gain. Position 0 is not what decides it.

Expected tokens per step is the sum of these probabilities plus the one token the target emits regardless. Accept length is the area under the curve, plus one. Nothing else enters it.

Depth is the axis the plane cannot show. MTP at n=3 caps at 4.00 tokens per step by construction and reaches 3.31. DFlash2's positions 3 through 6 add 1.18 tokens that MTP has no way to earn. The design brief for a replacement drafter is therefore not "beat 87 % once" but "hold 75–80 % over seven positions" — a far harder thing.

Why DFlash2 is cheap despite being large

Its drafter is 3.6 GB, larger than DSpark's 2.6 — yet it costs 26.2 GB per step against DSpark's 41.5. The reason is in the code, not the card: _generate_draft calls the draft model once and reads out every position from that single pass. DSpark runs its drafter once per position, seven times. Weights read per step are 22.6 + 3.6, not 22.6 + 7×3.6.
